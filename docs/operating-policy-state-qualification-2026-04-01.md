# Operating Policy Draft — State-by-State Activities (April 1, 2026)

This draft captures the operating stance discussed on April 1, 2026 for a
Delaware corporation managing domestic foreign-qualification risk.

## Core posture

- Treat out-of-state activity as **remote licensing + interstate sales** by
  default.
- Treat in-state implementation/labor as a **state review trigger**.
- Do not assume labeling work as "deployment" avoids qualification, nexus, or
  registration analysis.

## Practical operating rule

> Keep the company in one real operating state, and treat every other state as
> sales-only unless legal/tax review approves a state-specific exception.

## Draft board / sole-director policy sentence

> Global AVC Systems, Inc. will treat activity outside its designated operating
> state as remote licensing and interstate sales only, unless the sole director
> approves in-state implementation, labor, payroll, or physical operations
> after legal and tax review.

## Operational controls (default)

1. Out-of-state work is remote license/support only.
2. No in-person deployment outside the designated operating state without prior
   written approval.
3. No out-of-state payroll or local office footprint by default.
4. Any contract language implying on-site installation/training/support in
   another state requires review.
5. Repeated in-state labor by employees or contractors is escalated before work
   starts.

## Risk framing (state examples)

- **Massachusetts:** comparatively explicit treatment where transacting
  business/usual place of business and performing labor can trigger
  registration analysis.
- **New York:** more facts-and-circumstances driven; solely interstate commerce
  is generally lower risk, while local, permanent, continuous, and regular
  activity raises risk.

## Internal implementation note

Use this internal shorthand in contracts and delivery plans:

> Out-of-state activity = remote license only, unless separately approved for
> state-law review.

## Caution

This document is an operations draft, not legal or tax advice. Final
qualification/nexus positions should be confirmed by licensed counsel and CPA
review.
