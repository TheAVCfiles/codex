# Drift Signals and Gate Hardening Doctrine

Date: 2026-05-08

## Status

This document defines how Global AVC Systems, The House, FounderOS, and StagePort identify behavioral drift inside founder/client rooms without turning governance into surveillance or punishment.

The purpose is not to score people as good or bad.

The purpose is to detect when room behavior deviates far enough from the agreed operating frame that a human-in-the-loop review is required.

## Core Law

> Drift is signal. It is not automatically guilt.

Repeated deviation may indicate one of two high-value states:

1. The person is unusually creative, high-signal, and worth a closer look.
2. The person is extractive, unsafe, or attempting to bypass the room.

Both require zoom-in.

## Store Exit Analogy

If all standard doors are watched, a thief may try the fire exit.

This does not mean fire exits should be removed.

It means the fire exit must remain available while also being protected against misuse.

A safety exit can be exploited when someone:

- props it open
- has the code
- bypasses alarms
- exits with unpaid property
- blocks the exit while claiming exit rights
- uses safety language to avoid payment or accountability

The governance rule:

> Preserve the exit. Instrument the misuse. Protect the assets.

## What The House Should Detect

The House should flag patterns such as:

- repeated post-signature renegotiation
- refusal to acknowledge payment states
- repeated attempts to reframe scope after induced performance
- unusually high screenshot/export behavior before payment completion
- attempts to separate visible system design from licensing terms
- attempts to claim non-delivery after reviewing work product
- pressure for access before agreement or payment state is current
- requests for refunds while retaining observed architecture
- statements suggesting intent to rebuild protected work independently
- repeated confusion between exit rights and reuse rights
- failure to move through phase gates in good faith

## Drift Classes

| Class | Meaning | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creative Drift | Novel use case, unexpected insight, constructive deviation | Human review; may expand scope by change order |
| Confusion Drift | Misunderstanding of terms, scope, or payment | Education; written clarification |
| Panic Drift | Anxiety-driven overreach, excessive messaging, pressure | Slow down; state room status; reduce ambiguity |
| Extraction Drift | Repeated attempts to obtain value without payment/license | Harden gate; pause work; issue IP boundary notice |
| Obstruction Drift | Blocking payment, exit, or operating infrastructure | Pause or close room; preserve evidence |
| Compliance Drift | Behavior conflicts with platform, payment, or partner policy | Escalate; align with provider terms; document record |

## Human-in-the-Loop Review

Automated systems can flag drift. A human operator determines meaning.

The system should not autonomously punish, blacklist, report, or accuse based on drift alone.

A human operator reviews:

- payment logs
- agreement state
- room state
- access history
- communications
- scope record
- induced performance record
- license status
- platform policy implications

## Gate Hardening Responses

When drift is detected, responses should escalate proportionally:

1. Clarify
2. Educate
3. Slow down
4. Require written confirmation
5. Require payment before further work
6. Move to change order
7. Restrict access
8. Pause work
9. Close room
10. Preserve evidence and issue license/IP notice

## Education Before Punishment

Many founders are not malicious. They are under-trained in business ethics, contracts, payment norms, and IP boundaries.

The system should first teach:

- negotiation occurs before signing
- changes require change orders
- review access is not ownership
- payment gates are activation boundaries
- exits do not grant reuse rights
- partial activation does not authorize full implementation
- third-party eligibility cannot be guaranteed

## Phase Gate Qualification

Each phase is a review opportunity.

A client does not automatically qualify for the next phase merely because they completed a prior phase.

Qualification may consider:

- payment behavior
- respect for scope
- clarity of communication
- willingness to sign clean terms
- capacity to fund next phase
- non-extractive conduct
- operational seriousness
- partner/platform readiness

## Stripe / Payment Analog Review

Payment systems create operating records.

The House may review:

- payment completion
- failed payment patterns
- chargeback risk
- refund requests
- disputed payment behavior
- late payment behavior
- invoice aging
- metadata consistency
- customer communication around payment

This review must remain bounded and business-relevant.

## Low-Surveillance Principle

The House should prefer aggregate, state-based, and business-relevant records over invasive surveillance.

Track:

- room state
- payment state
- agreement state
- access state
- license state
- event type
- timestamp
- operator note

Avoid unnecessary collection of sensitive personal data.

## Fire Exit Abuse Rule

A safe exit is required.

But a safe exit does not permit:

- theft
- reverse engineering
- unpaid reuse
- IP laundering
- obstruction
- nonpayment
- post-performance reset

## Hydrant Violation State

A Hydrant Violation occurs when a client blocks or bypasses safety infrastructure required to protect the room.

Examples:

- refusing payment while demanding continued access
- demanding refund after induced performance
- attempting to copy system architecture while claiming exit rights
- refusing a change order while changing scope
- obstructing closure or evidence preservation

Recommended state:

> Room Paused — Hydrant Review

## Client-Safe Copy

> Your exit remains available. Reuse, implementation, reverse engineering, and continued access remain governed by the paid written scope. The room is paused while we review the activation and license state.

Or:

> This is a governance checkpoint, not a personal accusation. The next step is written clarification of payment, scope, and license status.

## Operator-Safe Copy

> Do not argue emotion. State the room state.
>
> Do not renegotiate verbally. Issue a change order.
>
> Do not keep working unpaid. Pause the room.
>
> Do not accuse. Preserve the record.

## Product Implication

The House should add flags for:

- Drift Review
- Hydrant Review
- Extraction Risk
- Change Order Required
- License Clarification Required
- Payment Gate Hardened
- Access Restricted
- Room Paused
- Room Closed

## Final Compression

The system must remain humane without becoming naïve.

Exits stay open.

Hydrants stay clear.

Payment gates stay boring.

Creative drift gets studied.

Extraction drift gets contained.
