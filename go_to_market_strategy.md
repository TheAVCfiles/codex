# Global AVC Systems — Early Client & Pricing Strategy

## First Client Segment

**Start with university movement science labs.**

Why this segment is ideal:

1. They actively adopt instrumentation.
2. They buy early-stage research tools.
3. They publish papers that validate tool credibility.
4. Their adoption creates institutional trust faster than marketing.

A single published lab paper can create more credibility than multiple promotional campaigns.

---

## Simplest Early Sale

**Offer:** _Global AVC Systems Architecture & Instrumentation Brief_

**Deliverables:**

- System design
- Measurement framework
- Pilot demonstration
- Dataset generation proposal

**Initial price range:** **$7,500–$20,000**

Position this as a blueprint and demonstration package, not a full product deployment.

---

## Early GTM Reality

Early infrastructure companies typically do **not** sell software first.

They sell:

- Expertise
- System design
- Pilot instrumentation

Software hardens into the product after pilots prove operational value.

## Outreach Angle That Converts

Do **not** lead with software sales language.

Lead with a system briefing invitation:

> “We’re developing a measurement framework for choreography and movement evaluation. I’d value your perspective and would love to show you a short demonstration of the instrument.”

The first goal is not immediate close. The goal is hearing:

> “Could we test this with our students/dataset?”

That is the pilot entry point.

## Licensing Strategy to Prevent Dilution

### Core principle

License **evaluation access**, not implementation rights.

Clients may:

- Read
- Review internally
- Evaluate collaboration potential

Clients may not:

- Build from materials
- Derive or replicate methods
- Train staff to recreate system
- Publish or commercialize framework details

### Mandatory restrictions (all tiers)

- No reverse engineering
- No derivative systems
- No training use for replication
- No sublicensing
- No commercialization without separate agreement

### Footer recommendation

**“Confidential — Evaluation License Only — No Implementation Rights Granted.”**

## Strategic Fork: Consulting vs Infrastructure

### Consulting path

Revenue scales with founder time. Strong near-term income, weak long-term leverage.

### Infrastructure path

Revenue scales with system usage and data accumulation. Strong compounding value.

Long-term moat comes from:

1. Movement grammar
2. Scoring ontology
3. Dataset generated over time

---

## Protection Stack for Scale

1. **Server-side core engine** (never ship core logic locally)
2. **Layered architecture** (UI/API exposed, engine private)
3. **Service-model licensing** (subscribe to service, not software transfer)
4. **Dataset control** (aggregated/limited access)
5. **Versioned outputs** (`engine_version`, timestamp, record hash)
6. **Invention trail documentation** (design logs, version history)
7. **Selective partner choice in pilots** (favor collaborators over copiers)

---

## North-Star Rule

For each engagement, ask:

> **Does this strengthen the platform (data, grammar, adoption), or only consume founder time?**

Use consulting as the bridge; build infrastructure as the engine.
